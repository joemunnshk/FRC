# Research notes — batch of new IPs (Nov 2026)

Working notes from research agents. Not published; kept so the facts survive.
Values marked "estimate" are the agent's ballpark, not Beckett/PSA data.

## Live-action film

**Lara Croft — 1997 Comic Images Tomb Raider 2 ClearChrome boxed set, card #1.**
7-card sealed set alongside Tomb Raider II. Predates the 1999 Precedence CCG and
the 2001 Inkworks film set. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM — single source (nslists.com), no
images found, TCDB 403. Fallback if it collapses: 1999 Precedence Tomb Raider CCG
Premier (nine different Lara cards). Value estimate: sealed set $15–50.
- https://nslists.com/tombrai2.htm
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_Raider_Collectible_Card_Game
- https://nslists.com/tombrmov.htm

**Neo — 2024 Kakawow Phantom Warner Bros 100th #PW-B-127.** Released 4 Oct 2024.
The Matrix went ~25 years with NO cards: Decipher announced a set in 2003 and
cancelled it at Warner's request (ICv2, 25 Mar 2003). My brief's premise of a
1999 Topps/Inkworks set was wrong. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM — TCDB lists a "2023 The
Matrix" set that could not be read (403); a McFarlane Movie Maniacs pack-in card
from 2024 is also unresolved.
- https://tradercracks.com/2024-kakawow-phantom-warner-bros-100th-anniversary-trading-cards-checklist
- https://icv2.com/print/article/2528
- https://upperdeck.com/?p=11544

**Marty McFly — 1985 Panini Back to the Future stickers (180).** Issued by
Minicards (UK), Scanlens (AU), O-Pee-Chee (CA), Panini (US). There is NO 1985
Topps BTTF set — Topps' first was 1989 Part II. A rival 1985 Kellogg's 8-card
cereal set exists (Canada, bilingual backs). CONFIDENCE MEDIUM — year solid,
but which 1985 issue shipped first is unknown, and Marty's sticker number is
unverified, so do not publish a number.
- https://csgb.co.uk/cardoftheday/2023-10-18
- https://www.cardboardconnection.com/1989-topps-back-to-the-future-ii-trading-cards

**Katniss Everdeen — 2012 NECA The Hunger Games #2.** 72-card base set, March
2012. #1 is a title card, #2 is Katniss. Jennifer Lawrence auto A1 was Walmart
midnight-release only, ~1:50. CONFIDENCE HIGH.
- https://nslists.com/hungame.htm
- https://www.cardboardconnection.com/2012-neca-hunger-games-trading-cards

## Blockers
TCDB (tcdb.com) and laststicker.com are both behind Cloudflare and return 403 to

## Nintendo / Pixar / Disney classic

**PUBLISH NOW:** Tom Nook, Marth, Nemo, Sulley, Mr. Incredible, WALL-E (year
only, no sticker number), Minnie, Pluto.
**HOLD:** Wario (low confidence), Goofy (year genuinely disputed), Fox McCloud

**Tom Nook — 2002 Nintendo Animal Crossing-e Series 1 #004.** The e-Reader
cards beat the 2015 amiibo cards by thirteen years. Japanese Dōbutsu no Mori+
Card-e ran Feb–Oct 2002 (335 cards); the NA line ran Oct 2002–May 2003 (328).
Nook is #004, after K.K. Slider, Rover and Porter. Serial 49120A 04-A004;

**Marth — 2004 NTT Publishing Fire Emblem TCG Series 6 #6-001.** Art by
Katsuyoshi Koya. Series 1–4 adapted Genealogy/Thracia, games Marth is not in,

and often mis-sold as first. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM-HIGH on the year; a Series 5
(Mar 2004) Marth may exist, same year.
- https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Fire_Emblem_Trading_Card_Game

**Nemo — 2003 Artbox Finding Nemo FilmCardz #02.** 72-card set; #01 is Marlin.

- https://nslists.com/findinfc.htm

**WALL-E — 2008 Panini WALL•E sticker collection (216).** No US card set exists;
House of Checklists has no WALL-E entry. DO NOT publish a sticker number.
CONFIDENCE MEDIUM.

**Sulley — 2001 Topps Monsters, Inc. #9 "Sulley's Number One".** 50-card set,

**Mr. Incredible — 2004 Upper Deck Disney Pixar Treasures #DPT-83.** 100-card

**Goofy — DO NOT PUBLISH A YEAR.** The R161 "Walt Disney Comics" anonymous strip

fallback: 1991 Impel / 1992 SkyBox Disney Collector Cards, but almost certainly
not his first.
- https://nslists.com/discomic.htm

pictured on the front. No Japanese Carddass/Amada Wario card found, but the
Japanese-language search was cut off.

**Fox McCloud — 1993 Nintendo Power Super Power Club #68 "Star Fox".** Year is

from a separate 1997 Star Fox 64 set — four years later, often mislabelled.

## Japanese / anime

**HEADLINE: Astro Boy does NOT unseat the 1935 Mickey Mouse.** The character was
created in 1951, so no Atom card can predate it.

**Astro Boy — 1959 live-action Tetsuwan Atom menko.** The TV drama ran 7 Mar
1959 – 28 May 1960 (65 eps, MBS/Fuji). Surviving menko carry photos of child
actor Segawa Masato, which is what pins the date — the rookie Atom is a human in
a costume, not the cartoon. Illustrated Atom menko plausibly run 1953–58 but are
undated and unmarked. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM; best-supported range 1953–1960, 1959
the only defensible integer. Many survive as uncut sheets (未裁断).
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鉄腕アトム_(実写版)

**Ultraman — 1966–67 broadcast-era bromides and kaku-menko** (Amada 天田印刷加工
and Yamakatsu 山勝 attested). Premiered TBS 17 Jul 1966, 39 eps to 9 Apr 1967.
Dating tool: period sets show only Bemular, Neronga, Ragon, Magular — episodes
1, 3, 4, 6. Once Seven/Returned/Ace characters appear it is 1971+ repackaging.
CONFIDENCE MEDIUM — no specific maker or printed date on a specific 1966 product.
The brief's "Kalbee" claim is wrong; Calbee's Kamen Rider Snack cards are 1971.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraman_(1966_TV_series)

**Doraemon — c. 1979–80 dagashiya mini-cards / menko, Amada attributed.**
CONFIDENCE LOW-MEDIUM — the year is inferred from the company the cards keep in
dagashiya lots (Goranger 1975, Dokaben 1976), not from any dated product. A
1973–75 card tied to the Nippon TV anime is plausible. Doraemon missed the menko
golden age: by 1970 the trade had shifted to thin mini-cards, so his rookie is a
card, not a true menko. Range 1973–1980.

**Rei Ayanami — Carddass Masters Evangelion 第壱集 (Bandai), 31 Aug 1996.**
172 cards, ¥300 for 10. Shinji and Unit-01 debut in the SAME set, so no date
separates them; Rei is picked because her singles are individually traceable.
No Eva card predates the Oct 1995 broadcast. First printing = blank space above
the Bandai logo at lower right of the pack; later runs print 第2版/第3版 there.
Bandai reprinted all 172 in July 2021 and said the reprints are NOT the same
designs. CONFIDENCE HIGH.
- https://evacollector.com/eva-no1242-cdm1-box/
- https://www.eva-info.jp/13316

**Eren Yeager — 2013 Siegkrone Shingeki no Kyojin starter (GREE), 26 Sep 2013.**
A TCG game card, not a collectible card. Boosters carried serial codes for GREE's
mobile game. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM — no checklist confirming Eren is in the starter,
so starter-vs-booster (14 Nov) is open, and an unverified "2013 wafer card" could
predate it.
- https://www.inside-games.jp/article/2013/06/25/67853.html

**Izuku Midoriya — 2016 Boku no Hero Academia Tag Card Game HAD-01 starter
"team 緑谷出久" (Takara Tomy), 23 Apr 2016.** Correction: the manga began 7 Jul
2014, not 2015. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM — a Jump Festa 2016 (Dec 2015) or World Hobby
Fair (Jan 2016) promo could move it to 2015.
- https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/201604049493

**Ichigo Kurosaki — 2002 Jump Festa Special WJ Card Set No.18 BLEACH
(Shueisha), on sale 22 Dec 2001.** Bleach debuted in WSJ Aug 2001; this is four
months later and three years before the anime. The sets numbered cards by
serialization seniority, so Bleach's number tracks its rise: No.18 (JF2002),
No.15 (JF2003), No.9 (JF2005). CONFIDENCE MEDIUM-HIGH.
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジャンプフェスタ

**Light Yagami — DO NOT PUBLISH the 2003 claim.** Candidate is 2004 Jump Festa
Special WJ Card Set No.17, on sale 20 Dec 2003 — but that rests on one
403-blocked catalogue entry, the artwork showing Light is inferred not seen, and
sellers routinely mislabel "Jump Festa 2004" for the festa held in 2004 (=
JF2005). SAFE FALLBACK: 2005 Jump Festa WJ Card Set No.13, 18 Dec 2004, well
attested. CONFIDENCE LOW.

**Schema note:** Eren, Deku and Marth are TCG game cards, not collectible-only
trading cards. Worth flagging if we ever distinguish them.

## Tier 1

**Scorpion — 1994 Classic Mortal Kombat (First Series) #7.** Classic Games Inc.
held the Midway licence; 100-card base set from digitised arcade sprites, ~18
months after the Aug 1992 arcade debut. Scorpion is on 20 of the 100 cards,

**Slimer — 1988 Topps Fright Flicks sticker #10.** THERE WAS NEVER A 1984 TOPPS
GHOSTBUSTERS SET — nobody issued cards for the original film until Cryptozoic in
2016. Fright Flicks was a multi-licence horror set (Alien, Elm Street, Predator,
Poltergeist…); Slimer got one of its 11 stickers. The 1984 film never names him
— "Slimer" was coined for the 1986 cartoon, so his first card postdates his name
by two years while using 1984 imagery. CONFIDENCE MEDIUM-HIGH. Open threat: a
Diamond "Real Ghostbusters" album carries a 1986 copyright but is catalogued
1988; every database that could settle it blocks fetching.
STAY PUFT: earliest confirmed is 2016 Cryptozoic #48. Fright Flicks #60 ("I
Wanna Roast Me a Boy Scout!") is probably him but unconfirmed — do not publish.
- https://www.cardboardconnection.com/1988-topps-fright-flicks-trading-cards
- https://nslists.com/frightfl.htm

**He-Man — 1983 Figurine Panini (Italy) Masters of the Universe, 216 stickers.**

## PlayStation Collectible Cards — verification results

Sony ran the programme entirely IN-HOUSE. No card manufacturer was ever
involved and the printer has never been named. PSX 2015: 130,000 cards across
35 designs, 66 distinct faces once rare parallels are counted. Commons used
bronze foil and were run 5–10x the holo rares, which carry an asterisk after
the card number. Distribution: RFID badge scans at booths, demo hands-on,
panels and signings, a Bronze Trophy card in every gift bag, app-tracked
quests, and the Road to Greatness tour truck. The rare Paragon card was hand
-distributed by five SCEA/Epic staff holding 100 each — about 500 copies, the
firmest rarity figure in the programme.

MARKET: roughly 50–65 active eBay listings worldwide. ZERO PSA graded
population for the numbered 2015–2018 cards. The SEA 2018 set has no market
presence at all. Ordinary single asks $13–20, honest floor nearer $8;
"10 Years of Uncharted" #087 asks $40–50. All ASKING prices — eBay now puts
sold listings behind a sign-in wall, so there is no realised-price data.

**PUBLISHED:** Aloy (2016 Series 2 #045) and Connor (2017 Series 3 #089).

**HELD — Joker / Persona 5 (2016 Series 2 #056).** The claim rests on beating
Bushiroad's Weiss Schwarz Persona 5 set by thirteen days (3 Dec vs 16 Dec
2016), and the Bushiroad product page 404s — every ws-tcg.com URL tried
returns 404, so the date cannot be verified. There is also residual risk from
WS promo cards P5/S45-P01 and -P02, which Japanese events sometimes hand out
pre-release. Image is NOT kept; re-cut from
https://psxcards.weebly.com/2016---set-2.html if this is ever resolved.

**HELD — Astro Bot (SEA 2018 #008).** The agent explicitly would not publish
it as settled: every Japanese secondary-market database (Suruga-ya, Mandarake,
Yahoo Auctions) was blocked, and a 2013–2016 Japanese PS4-launch promo card is
exactly what would surface there.

**DISPLACED — these PSX cards are NOT firsts:**
- **Kratos** — GamesMaster "Legends Combat Cards", Feb 2016, Future plc (UK),
  a 30-card Top Trumps-style boxed set cover-mounted with GamesMaster #300.
  Card reads "KRATOS / GOD OF WAR" with stats (Strength 10, Speed 5, Smarts 2,
  Charisma 1, Cuteness 1). Beats PSX #083 by 22 months. The 2010 official God
  of War III 3-card art set does NOT show Kratos — it is Helios, Hades and a
  centaur. NEEDS AN IMAGE.
- **Nathan Drake** — PSX 2014 collectible playing-card deck, Las Vegas 6–7 Dec
  2014, Ace of Spades is Uncharted 4 Drake key art. One year before #007.
  CATEGORY CAVEAT: a 54-card playing-card deck, not a trading-card set,
  though PSA has slabbed individual faces. Sony's Dan Hsu: because PSX 2014
  was in Las Vegas "it made sense for us to give out playing cards as
  collectibles". NEEDS A DECISION and AN IMAGE.
- **Ratchet & Clank** — foil bonus card packed in Ratchet & Clank 4th "SPECIAL
  GIFT PACKAGE", PS2 Japan, SCPS-15099, 23 Nov 2005. Ten years before #009.
  CATEGORY CAVEAT: a pack-in card printed with cheat commands on the reverse,
  not from a numbered set.
- **Wander / the Colossi** — four official art cards packed in the PAL PS2
  release of Shadow of the Colossus, 17 Feb 2006 (SCEE). Twelve years before
  #088. CATEGORY CAVEAT: publisher art cards, unnumbered.

**CHECKLIST DISCREPANCY:** psxcards.weebly.com puts Set 2 at #036–074 and Set
3 at #075–112, but Sony's own blog assigns #036/#037 to Series 1 and starts
Series 3 at #077. Does not affect #045, #056 or #089, but boundary cards need
a footnote.

## 2012 PlayStation All-Stars Battle Royale playing cards — GameStop Italia

**CONFIRMED BY SONY'S OWN BLOG.** blog.it.playstation.com, 15 Oct 2012, posted
by Maurizio Quintavalle, Web Manager SCE Italia:

> "Solo da GameStop, prenotando PlayStation All-Stars Battle Royale per PS Vita
> o per PS3, oltre ai costumi aggiuntivi per ciascun personaggio, avrete in
> omaggio un mazzo di carte personalizzato con **cinque protagonisti del
> gioco**."

A custom playing-card deck with FIVE of the game's characters, free with a
GameStop Italia pre-order. Game released 21 Nov 2012. Independently corroborated
on the Collector's Edition forums, 26 Oct 2012: "Gamestop Italy is offering an
exclusive deck of playing cards when you preorder this game."

The faces are an ordinary deck (a standard King of Hearts and Joker are
photographed); the five characters are printed on the BACKS.

**IDENTIFIED SO FAR (2 of 5):**
- **Nathan Drake** — the card facing out in Sony's official product photograph
  (https://blog.playstation.com/uploads/sites/12/2012/10/psasbr_mazzo_carte_gs.jpg).
  PUBLISHED.
- **Sly Cooper** — photographed on the Retro Gaming Shop listing. The gold
  hook-ended cane and masked raccoon face are unmistakable. PUBLISHED.
  (I first misread this card as Sir Daniel Fortesque.)

**THREE STILL UNIDENTIFIED.** Sony's press image is only 411x526, so the four
cards behind Drake cannot be resolved — one has a large red/orange form, one a
dark slim weapon with a blue spark, one is brightly coloured. Candidates from
the roster: Kratos, Sweet Tooth, Fat Princess, Nariko, Raiden, Cole MacGrath,
Jak, Toro, PaRappa, Sir Daniel Fortesque, Radec, Spike.

If those three include Kratos, Jak, Sweet Tooth or Toro, each gets a 2012 first
card. UNRESOLVED and now closed: reddit.com is blocked to Claude in Chrome by
policy and its JSON endpoint serves an interstitial, and the user has said not
to pursue it further.

Also checked: BlueDeepDive's "Playstation All Stars Battle Royale: Poker Cards
(Commentary) (PS3)" (youtube.com/watch?v=QdrwJucHHzc, 2013, 91 seconds, from a
17-video "PlayStation All-Stars: Collectables" playlist). It does NOT resolve
the three. The footage is handheld and too blurry to read a card, and its
thumbnail is just Sony's own press image upscaled. Two things worth recording:
the thumbnail carries "ESCLUSIVA GameStop" branding, and the video calls them
"Poker Cards". Three commenters call the VIDEO an April Fools gag — it went up
around 1 April and the channel ran jokes — but that is about the video, not the
product. THE DECK IS REAL: Sony's own blog announced it and the Retro Gaming
Shop listing photographs a physical copy, both independent of this video.

**MARKET:** essentially none. One sold-out Retro Gaming Shop listing at €44,90.
eBay.it returns nothing for the deck.

## Horror / Addams / GPK wave

**Fright Flicks is a dead end for all three slashers** — the full 90-card + 11
sticker checklist covers fifteen films and holds no Halloween, Friday the 13th
or IT licence. The answer for two of them is the 1992 Comic Images / Starlog
**Fangoria** set, which reproduces the magazine's covers as card fronts.

**CHECKLIST TRAP, again:** Fangoria base card **#60 is titled "Jason" and
pictures Critters 2.** Two sellers list it as "Jason Critters 2 Card #60".
Jason's actual card is the Chromium chase insert #6C. Also #64 is titled
"Halloween" but pictures Halloween III, the one film with no Michael Myers in
it, and #63 "Savini" shows Tom Savini rather than Jason.

**PUBLISHED:** Gomez Addams (1964 Donruss #17, Canadian printing — his own
title card is #1 but no copy was on the market), Adam Bomb (1985 GPK OS1 #8a),
Michael Myers (1992 Fangoria #51), Jason Voorhees (1992 Fangoria Chromium #6C).

**HELD — Pennywise.** There appears to be NO licensed Pennywise trading card
from either the 1990 miniseries or the 2017 film — only playing-card decks
(Paladone, Aquarius) and Garbage Pail Kids parodies. The earliest card art
depicting him is 2017 Topps GPK Halloween 7a "Benny Wise" / 7b "Tim Fury", an
online exclusive of 272 sets. That is a caricature under a different name, so
it fails the "first card for this character" test. Worth a second pass — a
major horror icon with no licensed card is itself a finding.

**Michael Myers licence note:** the Fangoria licence is the magazine's, not the
film's, which is why Fright-Rags could still bill its 2019 set as the first
officially licensed Halloween trading cards.

## Games wave

**PUBLISHED:** Solid Snake (1987 Konami Famicom pack-in card — eleven years
before the 1998 MGS set; lavender marbled stock, "© KONAMI 1987 TM", depiction
read off a published photo) and the Among Us Crewmate (2021 MIRA HQ

**CRASH BANDICOOT — PARKED, but with a better lead than the research had.**
The agent proposed 1998 Japanese sets (Kabaya gummi shokugan, Bandai Sealdass)
with the 2000 Jr. Sense "Crystal Crash" set as a conservative fallback. eBay
turns up something earlier and Western: a **Bluebird "Crash Bandicoot 2:
Cortex Strikes Back" collector card series**, 16 cards, carrying both the
PlayStation and Bluebird logos, which sellers date 1997. Crash 2 launched in
Europe on 7 November 1997, so a 1997 Bluebird tie-in is plausible. I have a
clean image of a single card (archived as crash-bluebird-orig.jpg).
BLOCKER: no source outside eBay documents the Bluebird set. crashmania.net has
no cards page (its /collectibles/cards/ URL serves a 404 body under a 200).
Needs one independent source before publishing; sellers list the same cards as
both 1997 and 1998, and the cards carry no printed date.

**JILL VALENTINE — HELD.** Leon cannot win (debuts Jan 1998). Jill's candidate
is Bandai's 1997 Carddass Masters「ALL CAPCOM WORLD」, but the agent flagged
depiction as INFERRED only, no single source ties Jill to the 1997 series
rather than the separate "'98" set, and every year attestation comes from
marketplace sellers. The widely quoted "#164" is unresolved — do not publish it.

**LEGO MINIFIGURE — HELD.** Candidate is the 1993 LEGO System Builders Club
collectable cards (BrickLink cc93lbc1–cc93lbc9), but depiction is INFERRED:
the cards are named for LEGO sets and nobody has confirmed a minifigure is
visible on any of them. The agent recommended holding until someone eyeballs a
scan, and could not rule out an earlier 1980s European club or cereal card.

## Anime and British/European waves

**PUBLISHED:** Totoro (NO CARD), Gojo (2021 Bandai wafer), Anya (2022 Ensky
clear card #02), Dalek (1965 Cadet Sweets #42), Wallace (1995 Merlin #106),
Paddington (NO CARD).

**TOTORO — no trading card exists.** Ghibli kept him out of the card market.
Tokuma's own 1988–95 merchandise catalogue lists telephone cards but no
trading cards, and nslists' anime index — which carries Akira, Ghost in the
Shell, Sailor Moon and Ranma ½ — has no Ghibli entry. **The "1988 Ensky Totoro
playing cards" sold everywhere is mis-dated: World of Playing Cards dates that
deck to 2010, and the ©1988 on the back is the FILM's copyright line. Ensky
did not exist as a separate company until 2005.** Also beware Chinese "Ghibli
CCG" boosters dated 2023–24 — no licensor named, unlicensed.
Nausicaä (1984) and Castle in the Sky (1986) have no card either.

**PADDINGTON — no licensed card exists.** The Cartophilic Society of Great
Britain went looking for one for Michael Bond's centenary and concluded he
"seemed never to have featured on a trade one". Only a 2018 Topps Wacky
Packages parody, which does not even use his name. Provisional: a 2014/2017
film tie-in album was not excluded.

**DOCTOR WHO year dispute:** PSA labels and part of the literature say 1965;
TARDIS wiki and SciFiFX say 1964. Published 1965 on the strength of the PSA
label I can actually show. Card 33 refers to The Dalek Book (Sept 1964), which
sets a floor only.

**WALLACE & GROMIT dating trap:** the "Merlin 1989" all over the market is the
copyright line from A Grand Day Out, not the issue year. The set is 1995.
Its published checklist names no subjects at all — "Sticker 1" to "Sticker
180" — so the PSA labels identify characters better than the checklist does.

**HELD:**
- **Smurfs.** 1982 Topps Smurf Supercards is a CEILING, not a floor: a 1980
  Belgian album (Les Schtroumpfs et nos champions) and a possibly-1975 sticker
  are unclosed leads. Depiction unconfirmed — no card is titled "Papa Smurf"
  at all, and #42 "Smurfettes Should be Treated Royally" is a Smurf-speak
  plural, not a portrait credit. NOTE: the Swedish Bulls Press Smurf swap
  cards carry NO manufacture date; the 1978/79 figures on eBay and PSA labels
  are submitter-supplied and cannot date a rookie.
- **Bluey (2024 Panini) and Peppa Pig (2013 Panini ref 002523).** Years look
  solid but I could not get an image of a single sticker — eBay matches "Blue"
  in football sticker listings and drowns both out.
- **Saint Seiya, Gon, Spike (Cowboy Bebop).** All three came back
  depiction-unconfirmed. Bebop is the dangerous one: its cards are titled by
  EPISODE ("Session #13"), so any number may picture anyone in that episode —
  the Sulley trap by design. Saint Seiya PART 1 is genuinely unnumbered.

## Toys / prestige TV wave

**PUBLISHED:** Daenerys (2012 Rittenhouse GoT Season One #66 — used the
self-identifying base card rather than the P1 promo, so no inference), Jack
Sparrow (Video Futur No.239, 2004), Tenderheart Bear (1985 Panini Care Bears;
I confirmed the depiction myself — the tan bear with the red heart belly badge
playing baseball on sticker #15).

**NEW SOURCE WARNING, worth carrying forward:** coleka.com's per-item "images"
for undocumented sets are AUTO-GENERATED PLACEHOLDERS — the album cover with a
number stamped on it. They look like scans and are not. This is the same trap
as the checklist titles.

**Two new useful databases:** klebebildchen.net (German sticker database:
publisher, year, sticker count, number ranges incl. lettered specials) and
coleka.com's browsable catalogues by publisher and year range.

**HELD:**
- **Eleven.** Her first card is the 2017 Loot Crate Stranger Things wax pack
  (11 cards + 2 stickers, "© 2017 NETFLIX / LOOTCRATE EXCLUSIVE"), a year
  before the 2018 Topps retail debut. Her card is titled "Pooling Our
  Resources" and the word Eleven never appears on it. The set is real — eBay
  carries singles titled "Late Night Hideout", "Hopper's Got A Hunch" and
  "Hangin' With Mr Clarke" — but no listing for HER card exists, and the
  review photos I pulled show the figurine and the shirt, not the cards.
  Conservative fallback if we give up: 2018 Topps Season 1 base #18 "Eleven",
  but that would misstate the year.
- **Applejack / My Little Pony.** 1986 Panini (180 stickers) vs the rival 1986
  Diamond album (225 stickers, red acetate decoder) — no source gives either a
  month, so which came first is unresolved. Depiction is confirmed on the
  ALBUM COVER only; the agent pulled stickers 1, 2, 3, 4, 96 and 103 and the
  European artwork is too loose to identify ponies by flank symbol.
- **Jack Sparrow caveat:** the year is the soft point. Coleka lists no year;
  2004 comes from the French video release plus neighbouring card numbering,
  which is only roughly chronological. A late-2003 issue cannot be excluded.
  Also an editorial call — it is a rental premium, not a packet-issued card.
